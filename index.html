Hammer Stand: DFM/DFA Redesign

ME 264, Introduction to Manufacturing for Mechanical Design · 2-person team

Dual-phase manufacturing project: fabricated a standard sheet-metal hammer stand, then ran a full DFM/DFA-optimized redesign built around a hinge-based support and a single additively manufactured bell tower assembly.

84 → 88DFA Score
$46.56Unit Cost (7% under budget)
9 StepsAssembly Time, 15–20 min
  • DFM
  • DFA
  • CNC Milling
  • Additive Manufacturing (FDM)
  • Engineering Drawings

Phase 1: Sheet Metal Baseline. Fabricated the original hammer stand through sheet-metal bending, hole drilling, and mechanical fastening. The real lesson wasn't the tooling, it was tolerance stacking: small bend-angle deviations compounded across the multi-part assembly, creating misalignment that only showed up at final fit-up.

Phase 2: DFM/DFA Redesign. Reworked the stand around a hinge-based hammer support and a combined bell tower / gusset bracket produced as a single additively manufactured part, constrained to a 2ft × 1ft × 1ft envelope and a $50 budget. The final design used 6 unique parts across CNC-milled 6061 aluminum and one ABS-printed component.

Design for Assembly. Ran a formal DFA evaluation across two design iterations, raising the DFA score from 84 to 88 by cutting fasteners and replacing a slide-in mount with a hinge for no-reposition assembly. The documented assembly sequence came down to 9 steps in roughly 15–20 minutes.

Cost and Manufacturing. Delivered the full assembly at $46.56 per unit, under the $50 cap, largely by combining the bell tower and gusset into one printed part and standardizing on M4 fasteners throughout, simplifying both the hardware bin and the CNC setups for the aluminum parts.

3D Electric Sports Car

Personal Project

Self-directed CAD study: taught myself Form, Fusion 360's surface-modeling toolset, with no course requirement behind it, and used it to design a complete two-door electric sports car from scratch.

5098 mmOverall Length, 1:1 Scale
100%Survey Design Approval
75%Would Buy As First Car
  • Fusion 360 Form
  • Surface Modeling
  • Product Design
  • User Research
  • Design Criteria & Evaluation

Self-Directed CAD Challenge. Designed a complete two-door electric sports car with four wheels, working proportions, lights, doors, mirrors, a charging port, and a continuous body surface with no visible gaps, using a surface-modeling tool I had never touched before starting.

Grounded In Research, Not Just Aesthetics. Modeled the car 1:1 against a BMW 5-series for reference proportions (5098 × 1420 × 1943 mm), and set the brief (target audience, price point, environmental angle) from researched sources before any modeling began.

Solving It Without A Tutorial. The hardest part was connecting tools, not learning any one of them: building a continuous side panel from the wheel arches meant finding the Bridge tool buried inside Modify, then deleting the leftover construction lines. Mirroring across the centerline kept the body symmetrical and cut the modeling work roughly in half.

Validated With Real Feedback. Two design teachers and a peer survey reviewed the finished model: 100% rated the design and its sportiness positively, and 75% said they'd buy it as a first car, with the roofline and door definition flagged as the clearest next refinements.

Sheet 03 · Siemens NX

Siemens NX Assembly Projects

CAD Coursework

Two full 3D assemblies built in Siemens NX, focused on dimensional accuracy, constraint-based assembly management, and modeling real mechanical motion.

2Full Assemblies
1:1Dimensional Accuracy
MotionKinematic Chain Modeled
  • Siemens NX
  • Assembly Navigator

Butterfly Valve Assembly. Modeled an industrial butterfly valve to its published dimensions, aiming for a true 1:1 representation instead of a rough approximation. Most of the difficulty was organizational: keeping a large set of components in order inside the Assembly Navigator and applying Touch and Align constraints so the body, seat, disc, and stem all resolved into one fully constrained assembly.

Little Blazer Engine Assembly. Built with function in mind from the start, so the model captures how the engine moves and not only how it looks. Constraints were set up so flywheel rotation drives the piston through the connecting rod and crankshaft, and the fuel tank was left free to slide along the base so its position stays adjustable.

Sheet 04 · Spring 2026

Three-Band Audio Equalizer

ECE 2007, Purdue University

Designed and built a three-band (bass, mid, treble) audio equalizer on a breadboard, from passive filters through to a speaker-driving amplifier stage.

3Bands: Bass, Mid, Treble
±10%Filter Cutoffs vs. Spec
LM386Power Amplifier Stage
  • Analog Circuit Design
  • Passive RC Filters
  • Op-Amp Buffers
  • Oscilloscope Measurement
  • Frequency Response Analysis

Signal path. Each band uses a passive RC filter followed by an op-amp buffer, with its own volume control, so the bass, mid and treble levels can be set independently. An LM386 power amplifier stage drives the speakers.

Validation. Measured the frequency response on an oscilloscope and confirmed that every filter cutoff frequency landed within ±10% of spec. One result deviated from spec, and I traced it back to its root causes.
